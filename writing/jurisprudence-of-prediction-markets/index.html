Enosinian Scholars Thesis

The Jurisprudence of Prediction Markets

2026 · Work in progress

Arik Karim

Abstract

In November 2020, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) approved Kalshi as the first fully-regulated financial exchange of event contracts, opening the legal floodgates for a nascent industry now exceeding $100 billion in annualized trading volume. In the last five years, the CFTC has reversed course on its rules on prediction markets, signaling agency uncertainty. Moreover, courts have since split on whether prediction markets are commodity derivatives or illegal gambling, whether federal law preempts state regulation entirely or merely coexists with it, and whether the insider trading doctrines forged in securities law have any purchase in a commodities exchange. My thesis maps the judicial decisions, statutory frameworks, and agency actions governing event contracts with the aim of clarifying the unsettled legal questions that prediction markets increasingly raise.
